## Persona 1 — Priya Raman, the inner-west renter

**Identity.** 31. Rents a one-bedroom in Marrickville with no off-street
parking. Product designer at a Barangaroo fintech, hybrid three days a week.
Income A$105k–120k. Drives a 2016 Mazda 2.

**Day in the life.** Leaves at 7:40am on school days to move the car off a
`No Parking 8–9:30am School Days` stretch near Marrickville West Public, then
takes the train. Evenings are Enmore Road and King Street: dinner, a gig, a
friend's place in Erskineville. Each trip ends under a pole of four plates,
photographing it "so I can argue later" and taking the space anyway. The
group chat has a running thread of sign photos captioned "???".

**Goals.** *Primary:* stop paying fines — $610 last year ($140 overstay twice,
$330 No Stopping once on Enmore Road at 4:05pm). *Secondary:* stop the 7:40am
move on days it turns out not to matter. *Underlying:* rent is 38% of take-home
and every fine comes out of the savings line.

**Pain points.**
1. Moves the car at 7:40am every weekday, including the two weeks of school
   holidays, because she cannot tell a school day from a Tuesday. *"I don't
   know when term ends. I just move it."*
2. Cannot tell which way the arrow applies. *"The sign was ten metres up the
   street. Apparently it pointed at me."*
3. Moves the car on public holidays when a Mon–Fri sign does not even apply.
   *"On Labour Day I moved it at 8am like an idiot. Nobody tells you the
   sign is off."*

**Current workarounds.** Camera roll (sign photos, never reviewed); a phone
alarm for the limit, set wrong when she misreads 1P as 2P; the Reddit search
bar. Cost: about 20 minutes a week of car-shuffling and roughly $12 a week in
amortised fines. None of it tells her whether today counts.

**Decision-making.** Discovers tools on r/sydney, Instagram Reels and the
group chat. Would try a link from a Reddit post that states the accuracy
number and shows the failures. Would pay $19.99 once, never a subscription,
on the day it saves her a $140 fine — but she is the grandfathered user, so
her value is the return visits and the wrong-reading reports. Nobody else in
the decision.

**Product fit.** Urgency **4** · Willingness to pay **3** · Reachability **5**
· **Primary target.**

## Persona 2 — Dave Kowalski, the sole-trader plumber

**Identity.** 44. Lives in Engadine, works inner-east, inner-west and CBD
strata jobs. Kowalski Plumbing: him, one apprentice, one Hiace, and a
bookkeeper (his wife, Ange). Gross A$180k–220k, net about A$110k. He is the
technical buyer; Ange is the economic buyer.

**Day in the life.** Six to eight jobs a day in ServiceM8, quoted on the
phone, invoiced from the van. Parks wherever the job is: a loading zone if he
can find one, a 1P if he thinks he will be quick, a No Stopping when the
burst pipe is worse than the fine. Phone in his pocket under a sink for two
hours, so no reminder reaches him. Notices come off the windscreen into a
folder Ange reconciles at BAS time.

**Goals.** *Primary:* fines under $500 this year; last financial year was
seven notices, about $1,500 ($330, $330, $235, $235, $140, $140, $140).
*Secondary:* stop losing ten minutes a job walking back to read the pole.
*Underlying:* he is pricing a second van, and every fine is an hour of
billable labour gone.

**Pain points.**
1. Reads a Sydney sign cluster wrong under time pressure. *"Loading zone,
   then a clearway plate above it. Which one wins at 3:30?"*
2. Never knows how long he has once he is inside. *"I'd set a timer if I
   knew what to set it to. And I'd need it to go off in my pocket."*
3. Cannot contest anything because the evidence is gone. *"By the time I see
   the ticket I've forgotten what the sign said."*

**Current workarounds.** Gut feel; the apprentice minding the van; a
laminated "Commercial vehicle" sign that does nothing legally; "watch
parking" typed into ServiceM8 job notes. Cost: about $30 a week in fines and
45 minutes a week walking to and from poles. Appeals: none, ever.

**Decision-making.** Discovers tools in trade Facebook groups, at the Reece
counter, and from the apprentice. Would try it if the apprentice showed him
the 12-hour timeline on a usual street. Would pay for Fleet at
$4/vehicle/month the day the second van starts, provided the reminder fires
with the phone in his pocket — which it now does: the app's Web Push
reminders reach a closed tab once it is installed to the home screen. Ange
signs off; she will compare it to CellOPark's $1.99.

**Product fit.** Urgency **5** · Willingness to pay **5** · Reachability **2**
· **Secondary target.**

## Persona 3 — Megan Tran, the occasional CBD driver

**Identity.** 52. Lives in Erina on the Central Coast. Team leader at a state
government agency, income A$92k–100k. Drives to Sydney two or three times a
month: a specialist at RPA, her daughter's flat in Randwick, matinees at the
Theatre Royal.

**Day in the life.** Plans the trip around parking before anything else.
Books a Wilson Parking bay online for $45–60 because the streets near RPA
"all say something different every twenty metres". The one time she risked a
street in Randwick she got a $140 No Parking notice because she read the
weekend plate as weekday-only. Drives home on the M1 annoyed about the $60,
not the traffic.

**Goals.** *Primary:* park near the hospital without the $50. *Secondary:*
understand the signs once, so she stops relying on her daughter. *Underlying:*
she is not a confident city driver and does not want to feel foolish.

**Pain points.**
1. Does not know the vocabulary. *"1P, 2P, ticket, meter — what is the
   difference between a ticket and a meter?"*
2. Cannot tell what is in force right now. *"It says Mon–Fri and it's
   Saturday, so I'm fine? I genuinely do not know."*
3. Wants a plain answer, not a legal document. *"Tell me yes or no and when
   I need to be back."*

**Current workarounds.** Wilson Parking and Secure Parking bookings ($100–150
a month); her daughter on FaceTime pointing at the sign; Google searches for
"what does 2P mean NSW". Cost: about $30 a week and a phone call she would
rather not make.

**Decision-making.** Finds things by Google search, ABC Radio Sydney drive and
her daughter. Would try it if her daughter sent the link, or if a search for
"can I park here Sydney" landed on a page that answered it. Would pay $19.99
once, after the first trip where she parked on the street and did not get a
fine. Nobody else in the decision, but her daughter is the channel.

**Product fit.** Urgency **2** · Willingness to pay **3** · Reachability **3**
· **Tertiary target.**

## Persona priority matrix

| Persona | Urgency | WTP | Reachability | Total | Priority |
|:--|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--|
| Priya — inner-west renter | 4 | 3 | 5 | 12 | **Primary** |
| Dave — sole-trader plumber | 5 | 5 | 2 | 12 | Secondary |
| Megan — occasional CBD driver | 2 | 3 | 3 | 8 | Tertiary |

**Build for Priya first.** She is already on r/sydney and Reels, the two
launch channels in GO-TO-MARKET.md, and she is the only persona whose
behaviour moves all three of the retention metrics in section 5 there
(scan→timer rate, week-4 return rate, repeat scanners): she scans three to
four times a week, on stacked plates, in the inner-west school-zone and
public-holiday cases where the calendar engine is the whole difference. Her
wrong-reading reports and "did you get a fine?" answers are the accuracy
corpus the paywall gate depends on, and she is grandfathered, so there is no
conversion to worry about — only whether she comes back.

Dave has the same total and far higher willingness to pay, and the feature
he needs most — a warning that fires with the phone in his pocket — now
exists. What he lacks is a channel: he is reached by outbound sales and
trade groups, not a Saturday Reddit post. He becomes primary when Fleet is
built and the outbound list in GO-TO-MARKET.md §5 is worked.
Megan arrives on her own through the SEO articles once they rank; do not
spend launch effort finding her.
